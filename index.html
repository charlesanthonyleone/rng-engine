Every engine you've ever used assumes determinism: same input, same output, forever. RNG Engine flips that. True randomness, with no seeds and no rewind, is the ground everything stands on. Nothing happens the same way twice, and that's the point.

Executive summary · a design thesis, not a shipped engine

A universe is not
a replay file.

Most worlds are generated once and then operated. RNG Engine asks a larger question: what if becoming were the operating principle?

The proposition is a persistent, shared universe in which creation is an irreversible event. Fresh entropy enters at the substrate. Physics constrains the possible; chance chooses the particular. AI turns an intention into a candidate, validation makes it admissible, and a durable record makes it part of the world.

The business model follows the same distinction: a subscription funds access and a bounded allowance of generation. Additional complexity consumes more credits. People pay for the act of bringing something into being—not for a scarce item to flip.

The proof gate is one complete, playable, shippable game. This page is the architectural and commercial thesis. The animated bay illustrates its aesthetic and non-repeatable composition; it is not evidence that the engine, asset pipeline, physics system, or business has been built or validated.

The no-seed, no-rewind substrate

The apple is an event.

Plant the same kind of tree in the same kind of soil. You do not get the same apple back.

An apple is not merely an item identifier. It has a particular shape, history, bruising, weight, and relation to the branch that grew it. Another apple can share its category without being its copy. Once picked, that event happened. Recording it does not mean that time can be rewound to unpick it.

That is the inversion. Categories and conservation laws remain coherent; exact sameness stops being the default. The engine samples admissible outcomes from fresh entropy, then commits the chosen outcome. A saved world preserves what happened. It does not preserve a recipe that can regenerate the same future.

ξₜ ← fresh entropy
Sₜ₊₁ ∼ K( · | Sₜ, Aₜ, ξₜ )
Lₜ₊₁ = append(Lₜ, committed event)

S is world state, A is an action, K is a constrained transition kernel, and L is the durable event record. There is no public seed or reversible timeline. An event log records outcomes; it is not permission to undo them.

Random does not mean inconsistent. “Non-deterministic math” means sampling within a mathematical model—not randomly changing arithmetic, corrupting memory, or letting a clock run backward. Memory safety, causal order, conservation constraints, and accounting must remain reliable. The literal universal substrate is a research ambition, not an implemented capability.

True entropy is an engineering requirement. A production design would need an auditable physical-entropy source, conditioning, health checks, and a failure policy. Browser crypto.getRandomValues is a cryptographically strong randomness interface, not a guarantee of a direct physical RNG. It makes this scene hard to predict; it does not prove the full thesis.

No rewind is a world rule. Backups and disaster recovery are still necessary. Recovery must distinguish restoring a service from erasing player history. Debugging and audit tools may inspect recorded events without making the live universe replayable.

The engine, layer by layer

The Inverted Stack

Traditional engines treat randomness as a feature you call. Here it's the floor. Six layers, built bottom-up, each inheriting chance from the one beneath it.

Descend to the floor↓

01Abyssal floor · the ground

Substrate

Memory, math, and the clock itself are non-deterministic. Time doesn't tick; it rolls. This is the core assumption every other engine gets "right," and the one we replace.

ξₜ ← Eₚₕᵧₛ ; τₜ₊₁ > τₜFresh entropy enters; the logical clock still preserves causal order. Entropy never substitutes for memory safety.
02Midnight water · forks

Simulation

Physics, AI, and anything that ticks forward. Outcomes fork. Throw the same rock twice and you get two histories. Real physics, never replayed.

sₜ₊₁ ∼ Kₚₕᵧₛ( · | sₜ, aₜ, ξₜ )A transition is sampled within physical constraints. Random perturbation must not create free energy or impossible momentum.
03Twilight water · light arrives

Representation

Rendering and audio. A texture loads differently each time, a sound never plays the same. What you see is a moment, not a copy.

rₜ ∼ P( · | sₜ, viewpoint, ξₜ )Variation belongs inside a validated appearance envelope. Identity, collision boundaries, and accessibility cannot drift arbitrarily.
04Shallows · form takes shape

Structure

Scene graph, entities, relations. Sameness is inverted: no two instances are identical by default. Identity is earned, not assumed.

Gₜ₊₁ ∼ Kɢ( · | Gₜ, event, ξₜ )A world graph evolves; referential integrity and durable identifiers survive the roll. Similar objects are not identical instances.
05The shore · rules meet the tide

Behavior

Scripting and logic. Rules bend under probability. Code describes tendencies, and the world decides.

aₜ ∼ π( · | sₜ, intent, ξₜ )Scripts specify admissible tendencies. Hard rules, player consent, and safety limits remain non-negotiable constraints.
06The land · where people live

Social

Networking, persistence, player-to-player and player-to-world connections. Shared reality, built from rolls nobody can predict or rewind.

eₜ ← authority ; Lₜ₊₁ = append(Lₜ, eₜ)The server commits one event and distributes it. Clients do not independently roll contradictory shared realities.

Above the land

The Economy Layer

On top of the stack sits the economy: people pay to generate.

Generate and record.
AI creates assets, and the engine records them permanently into the universe. Base storage is paid for.
Subscription.
Around $15/month, including generation credits.
Price the verb, not the object.
You pay to create, not to own. No hoarding, no reselling, no speculation on items.

Tiered verbs.

  1. Small: props and objects. Cheap.
  2. Mid: characters and vehicles.
  3. Large: whole regions or rule changes. Expensive, and meant to be.

The cost is the filter. It keeps the universe from becoming a junk drawer of 18 trillion low-quality things, and it makes rare finds actually rare.

The Vision

A randomly generated universe where anything someone can imagine can be real, with real physics. Spaceships. Flying surfboards. Whatever you can name, the world can hold it and it behaves like it belongs there.

Not infinite noise. Infinite meaning, because every thing in it cost someone something to bring into being.

Robloxproved that creators and players can share a vast platform. RNG Engine would invert the authored-object model: creation becomes an irreversible world event, not a repeatable asset upload.

Unrealsets the professional bar for rendering, tooling, physics, and production. The proposal is to understand that stack deeply, identify where repeatability is assumed, and rebuild that layer around fresh entropy.

No Man's Skydemonstrates the scale and wonder of procedural space. RNG Engine aims for a different property: discoveries that are not regenerated from a seed, and creations whose rarity comes from cost and history.

These are reference points for scale and ambition—not claims of partnership, compatibility, endorsement, equivalent capability, or market position.

The standard · phased rebuild plan

The bar is simple: ship one full game on it.

The method: reverse-engineer Unreal Engine down to its single core assumption, determinism. Tear that out. Replace it with true RNG. Then rebuild every feature on top, layer by layer, until nothing is missing and nothing is the same.

  1. Phase 0

    Map the assumption

    Use permitted documentation, licensed source access, and clean-room analysis to map where repeatability is assumed: random streams, save systems, replication, physics stepping, tests, and asset identity.

    Gate: dependency map, licensing review, and measurable invariants.
  2. Phase 1

    Build the entropy kernel

    Create the entropy service, constrained sampling layer, logical clock, and immutable event record with explicit failure behavior.

    Gate: health tests, audit logs, and state integrity under failure.
  3. Phase 2

    Prove one bounded slice

    Build one small world and one complete loop: a player acts, physics samples an admissible outcome, the event commits, and every connected player sees the same result.

    Gate: stable playtests on supported hardware and networks.
  4. Phase 3

    Add generation and economy

    Connect intent-to-asset generation, validation, moderation, storage, and credit metering without creating resale incentives.

    Gate: cost per verb and quality thresholds meet the model.
  5. Phase 4

    Rebuild the feature stack

    Expand rendering, audio, AI, scripting, tooling, creator workflows, and persistence until the full game can be built without hand-waving.

    Gate: feature-complete content pipeline and production QA.
  6. Phase 5

    Ship the proof

    Release one complete game, measure retention and cost in production, and expand toward a universe only after the bar is met.

    Gate: public, supported, maintained release.

Risks and mitigations

Where the thesis can break.

A serious thesis states the failure modes before they find it.

01

Engineering correctness

Randomness can be mistaken for permission to corrupt memory, arithmetic, or timing. The architecture must keep integrity guarantees deterministic where safety requires them and randomize only admissible outcomes.

02

Testing and debugging

Non-repeatable worlds are harder to test. Use invariant tests, statistical acceptance tests, observability, and auditable event histories rather than relying only on exact replay.

03

Multiplayer consistency

Independent client rolls would split reality. An authoritative commit protocol, conflict handling, latency budgets, and anti-cheat controls are required.

04

Generation cost and quality

Asset generation, validation, moderation, and storage may exceed $15 per member. Metered verbs, usage caps, quality gates, and cost monitoring are mandatory.

05

Legal, licensing, and IP

Unreal is licensed technology. Reverse-engineering, engine changes, content generation, and distribution must follow applicable licenses and law; legal review belongs at the start, not after the build.

06

Safety and trust

Permanent generation can preserve harmful, infringing, or unwanted content. Moderation, takedown processes, privacy controls, and recovery policies must coexist with the no-rewind principle.
